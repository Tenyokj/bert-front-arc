## Funds and Entitlements

`totalPoolBalance` is recorded token custody, **inclusive** of protocol reserve
and uncollected Treasury revenue. Neither reserve nor total custody is team income.

Only success fees earned by an opted-in round create revenue. Deposits, direct
transfers, slashed bonds and every V3 reserve inflow retain their previous purpose.
The receiver never gains a percentage of token custody or grant principal.

At each fee vesting step:

```text
eligibleFee = min(totalFee, floor(recordedWinningPledges * roundFeeBps / 10_000))
eligibleEarnedFee = floor(cumulativeEarnedFee * eligibleFee / totalFee)
cumulativeRevenue = floor(eligibleEarnedFee * roundShareBps / 10_000)
newRevenue = cumulativeRevenue - revenuePreviouslyAccruedForRound
retainedReserve = newlyEarnedFee - newRevenue
```

The entire fee rate is still snapshotted by the existing round-opening flow.
The new share applies to that fee, not as an additional charge to the pledge.
Rounding uses cumulative fee vesting, not independent per-tranche rounding.
When eligibleFee is zero, eligibleEarnedFee is zero without division. Reserve
top-ups do not increase recordedWinningPledges, including allocations made before
opening the round. Existing grant/total-fee calculations remain unchanged; fees
attributable to reserve-funded subsidy remain in reserve rather than team income.
The eligible fee and total fee are fixed at settlement and publicly exposed in
the round's policy and `FundingRoundRevenueBaseSet` event.

Example only, **not approved deployment settings**: a 1,000-USDC winning pledge,
5% fee and 40% revenue share give a 950-USDC net grant and a 50-USDC total fee.
At the initial 20% grant payout, 10 USDC of fees vest: 4 USDC become Treasury
entitlement and 6 USDC remain reserve. The 40 unearned fee USDC remain refundable.
After complete delivery, Treasury revenue is 20 USDC and retained reserve 30 USDC.

Failed grants restore **unearned** fees under existing V2.3 refund rules. Revenue
earned on already paid tranches is not clawed back. Never-claimed grants create
no revenue and retain their full-gross refund path. Legacy reviewer rounds opened
under an enabled new policy retain their existing claim-time fee vesting rule.

## Policy and Trust Boundary

The default receiver/share are zero, including immediately after upgrading.
There is no revenue reinitializer and no retroactive assignment of old reserve.

Here, "revenue policy" means only the Treasury receiver and its share of earned
success fees, not ownership of the pool balance. For example, a 40% share of
5 USDC in eligible earned fees credits 2 USDC to Treasury; it does not entitle
Treasury to 40% of deposits, stakes, grant budgets or refunds.

The 48-hour delay is a safety design choice enforced by the deployed pool,
not an Arc requirement. The first scheduled policy is awaiting its deadline.
The owner approved this delay and the testnet split on 2026-11-24; actual receiver
deployment/constructor evidence and write authorization are still required.
Changing the constant requires a reviewed source
change and fresh tests. Development and local tests do not require waiting two
real days. Only an actual public schedule starts the public waiting period.

1. Admin calls `scheduleRevenuePolicy(treasury, shareBps)`.
2. The full **48-hour** public delay must elapse; replacing a proposal restarts it.
3. Anyone can execute the exact pending proposal with `executeRevenuePolicy()`.
4. `openFundingRound` snapshots receiver and share into `fundingRoundRevenue`.

Admin can cancel a proposal. A zero-address/zero-share policy disables revenue
for future rounds after the same delay. Valid nonzero shares range from 1 to
10,000 bps; the actual deployment share remains a separate product decision.
No receiver can change an opened round's policy or take another receiver's credit.

The receiver must report this pool and the same token. These identity getters
are **not a bytecode allowlist or independent security proof**. Administrators must
verify the deployed immutable Treasury implementation, constructor arguments,
beneficiaries and shares before scheduling it. A malicious or inaccessible
receiver can strand its own fee entitlement but is never called during vesting.

Scheduling the first valid nonzero receiver permanently pins the pool's token,
including after cancellation or disabling. This prevents the old token setter
from changing the asset behind historical round/receiver entitlements. The
compatibility `setGovernanceToken` setter obeys the same restriction.

**The delay applies only to revenue policy.** It does not add a timelock to every
existing Admin action. ProxyAdmin can still replace FundingPool code; this release
does not remove upgrade-owner trust or deploy a Safe/upgrade timelock.

## Collection and Beneficiary Payments

Fee vesting updates internal ledgers only. It does not call Treasury or transfer
tokens to beneficiaries, so a receiver outage cannot block a grant payment.

Anyone may call `treasury.collect(amount)`. Treasury calls
`pool.claimProtocolRevenue(amount)` and receives **only its own** earned credit.
The caller cannot supply an alternative destination. Collection is blocked while
FundingPool is paused and if actual custody is below the recorded total.
Pausing FundingPool does not freeze beneficiary payments from revenue already
collected into the separate Treasury; Treasury has no administrator pause switch.

Treasury distributes receipts to 1-16 unique fixed beneficiaries. Nonzero shares
must sum to 10,000 bps. Anyone may relay `release(payee)`, but payment always goes
to that beneficiary. A beneficiary can itself be a team multisig. There is no
owner, mutable recipient, arbitrary call, approval, rescue, or upgrade function.
Changing future beneficiaries requires a new Treasury and delayed pool policy;
old credits remain assigned to the old Treasury, never swept into the new one.

Treasury direct-USDC donations follow the same fixed split. They are not recorded
as FundingPool revenue. Beneficiary rounding dust stays in Treasury; later
receipts may make it payable. Unrelated tokens, lost beneficiary keys or blocked
USDC recipients have no administrator rescue. A failed beneficiary transfer
rolls back only that call and does not block other beneficiaries' separate claims.

Both sides require exact token debit/credit for revenue transfers. False returns,
reverts, reentry, recipient taxes and sender surcharges must not consume credit.
This is **not generic malicious-token safety** for the entire protocol: supported
settlement remains exact-transfer, non-rebasing USDC. Token issuer freeze and
blocklist powers remain external risks.

## Accounting View and Reconciliation

`getTreasuryAccounting()` reports:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| actual | Current USDC token custody |
| accounted | Recorded custody including reserve and earned credit |
| reserve | Existing protocol allocation reserve, not team revenue |
| revenue | Earned but uncollected receiver credit |
| otherFunds | Combined bonds, pledges, grant/fee escrow and ordinary deposits |
| surplus | Actual minus accounted when positive; never automatically revenue |
| shortfall | Accounted minus actual when positive |

The view rejects reserve/revenue totals exceeding recorded custody. It does not
invent full historical aggregate counters that did not exist before this upgrade.
Per-round pending fee getters retain their historical semantics: during vesting,
the pending fee value is a **total**, so subtract released fees when calculating
the remaining unearned escrow. Do not add both values as separate liabilities.

`syncBalance` previously assigned `actual - protocolReserve`, contradicting
deposit/payout paths which include reserve in total custody. The candidate assigns
the full actual balance, preserves every category and creates no revenue. It
rejects negative reconciliation instead of hiding a custody shortfall. This is a
bug fix, **not proof that all historical balances reconcile**.

The separate [pinned custody inventory](V2.4-HISTORICAL-ACCOUNTING.md) now explains
the actual 1,140-USDC snapshot using canonical receipts and state reads. Its
explorer-discovery limitations and fresh-preflight requirement still apply.

## Storage and Upgrade Rehearsal

Ten slots are appended into FundingPool's existing gap, reducing it from 29 to
19 slots. Existing fields, mappings, inherited storage and legacy structs stay
in place. Compiler `storageLayout` output is enabled for repeatable comparison.
The regression deploys the frozen 1.4.0 implementation behind an actual OZ v5
Transparent proxy, populates real grants, bonds, deposits and V3 reserve routing,
then upgrades and continues the old grant/refund lifecycle with zero revenue.
See [fixture provenance](https://github.com/Tenyokj/bert-core-arc/blob/arc-usdc-migration/test/fixtures/README.md).

The ordinary optimized FundingPool runtime is 24,269 bytes, only 307 bytes below
the 24,576-byte EIP-170 ceiling (solc 0.8.28, Prague, 200 optimizer runs).
ProtocolRevenueTreasury runtime is 3,123 bytes. Recheck this limit for every
source/compiler change; instrumented coverage bytecode is not a deployment build.

   not that live-state fork rehearsal.

   reviewed pool upgrade with appropriate maintenance controls. No revenue call
   is required in `upgradeAndCall`; default configuration is disabled.
7. Reconcile post-upgrade state. If separately approved, sync legitimate surplus,
   schedule policy, wait 48 hours and execute. Validate one new testnet round from
   pledge through all tranches, refunds, Treasury collection and beneficiary payout.
8. Record receipts, source commit, build hashes and versions. Keep old round
   snapshots and zero-credit old reserve intact. Do not blindly downgrade after
   enabling revenue: old code does not understand the new earmarked credit.
