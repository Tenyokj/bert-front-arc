# BERT V2.3: Backer-Governed Milestones

## Status

**Design specification. Not deployed.** V2.2 remains the live Arc Testnet release until
the V2.3 implementation, tests, storage validation, static analysis, and testnet upgrade
are complete.

## Purpose

V2.3 keeps V2's conditional-pledge funding model and replaces the global
`REVIEWER_ROLE` milestone gate for new grants with a decision made by the backers whose
pledges funded the winning idea. The goal is to preserve staged capital release without
assuming that an unpaid, protocol-wide reviewer market already exists.

V2 is a winner-takes-round funding primitive for a Community that intentionally chooses
one priority grant. It is not a quadratic-funding system, a retroactive funding program,
or a mechanism intended to distribute capital to every proposal.

## Terms

- **Pledger / backer:** a wallet that recorded a USDC pledge for an idea in a funding
  round. Public product copy should prefer these terms over "investor".
- **Winning pledge cohort:** the immutable set of pledgers whose pledges were recorded
  for the settled winning idea.
- **Pledge weight:** the USDC amount recorded for one member of the winning pledge
  cohort. It determines the member's milestone-vote weight.
- **Milestone plan:** an immutable evidence and acceptance-criteria package committed
  before a proposal can enter a funding round.
- **Proof package:** an immutable evidence package submitted by the author for one
  milestone. It may contain many off-chain links, but is represented on-chain by one URI,
  its content hash, and a concise summary.

## Preserved V2.2 Rules

1. A funding proposal declares `minimumNetFunding` before it can enter a round.
2. A pledge is economically attached to the selected idea, not redirected to a different
   winner.
3. The highest-ranked proposal that reaches its post-fee funding target wins. A
   non-viable higher-ranked proposal is skipped in favour of the next viable proposal.
4. Losing pledges are pull-refundable. If no proposal is viable, every pledge is
   refundable.
5. The direct author address cannot pledge/vote for its own proposal.
6. Production participation requires the production verified-human integration. The
   testnet demo verifier is not a Sybil-resistance guarantee.
7. The current successful-round protocol fee remains a policy value, snapshotted when a
   funding round starts. The V2.2 Arc policy is 500 basis points (5%).

## V2.3 Funding Eligibility

New V2.3 funding rounds last **14 days** by default. Pledgers need time to assess
the proposal, its threshold, and its committed delivery plan before capital is
escrowed.

When a V2.3 round starts, it snapshots both its V2.3 lifecycle mode and the configured
human-verifier address. Changing the global verifier or disabling global human-only
voting later cannot weaken an active V2.3 round. Production must configure a
production-grade PoP verifier before enabling this mode; the testnet demo verifier is
only suitable for functional testing.

A proposal is viable only when all of the following are true at round settlement:

1. Its gross winning pledge reaches `minimumNetFunding` after the round's snapshotted
   protocol fee.
2. Its winning pledge cohort contains at least three distinct verified-human backers.

The second condition is required because a grant governed by backers cannot safely start
with fewer than three eligible approve voters. If the highest-ranked proposal fails either
condition, settlement evaluates the next proposal in rank order.

## Grant Economics

### Tranche Schedule

The author-side net grant is released in this fixed schedule:

| Stage | Net grant share | Trigger |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Initial claim | 20% | Author claims before the claim deadline |
| In-process milestone | 40% | Winning backers approve the proof package |
| Completion milestone | 40% | Winning backers approve the proof package |

The current author-side time limits remain the baseline policy:

| Action | Window |
| --- | ---: |
| Claim a settled grant | 14 days after round settlement |
| Submit in-process proof | 45 days after initial payout |
| Submit completion proof | 60 days after in-process payout |
| Resubmit after a rejection | One time, after a 48-hour cooldown and before the stage deadline |

### Progressive Fee Vesting

V2.2 finalizes the whole successful-round fee at the first grant claim. V2.3 changes this
so that the fee vests alongside each released tranche.

For a gross winning pledge of 100,000 USDC and a 5% fee:

| Event | Author receives | Protocol reserve receives | Gross escrow remaining |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Initial claim | 19,000 | 1,000 | 80,000 |
| In-process approval | 38,000 | 2,000 | 40,000 |
| Completion approval | 38,000 | 2,000 | 0 |

If a grant stops after a stage, only the fee earned by already released tranches remains
in protocol reserve. The full gross value of unreleased tranches remains refundable to the
winning pledge cohort.

The existing 50 USDC author bond remains an anti-spam bond in V2.3. It is not represented
as insurance against execution failure. A proportional performance bond is deferred until
real grant data can calibrate its cost and effect on early-stage authors.

## Backer Milestone Voting

### Eligibility and Weight

1. Only members of the winning pledge cohort may vote.
2. Eligibility and pledge weight are read from the settled pledge record; no wallet may
   join the cohort after settlement.
3. The author is always excluded.
4. A backer has one active position, `approve` or `reject`, and may change it until the
   review deadline. The final position is counted.
5. Vote weight is the backer's winning pledge amount. This preserves alignment between
   economic risk and decision power.

### Decision Rule

For each submitted milestone proof, all conditions below must be satisfied at finalization:

1. **Quorum:** `approveWeight + rejectWeight` is at least 40% of the total gross winning
   pledge weight.
2. **Approval:** `approveWeight` is at least two-thirds of participating pledge weight.
3. **Human safeguard:** at least three distinct verified-human backers cast an approve
   vote.

The vote does not slice the grant by voter position. An approved milestone releases the
predefined tranche from the whole grant escrow. A rejected or expired milestone makes the
whole unreleased escrow refundable proportionally to every winning pledger, including
approve voters, reject voters, and non-voters.

### Timeline and Outcomes

1. The author submits a proof package. The main review window is 14 days.
2. The UI and notification services should notify the entire winning cohort at submission,
   midway through review, and shortly before expiry.
3. The vote remains open for the full window even if quorum is reached early. This gives
   backers time to inspect proof and change a mistaken vote.
4. If the decision rule is met at expiry, anyone may finalize approval and release the
   next tranche.
5. If quorum is not met after 14 days, the same proof receives one 7-day grace window.
6. If quorum is still not met at the end of the grace window, the grant is cancelled and
   the unreleased escrow is activated for pull refunds.
7. If quorum is met but approval fails, the milestone is rejected. The author may submit
   one corrected proof package after the cooldown.
8. A second rejection or missed author submission deadline permanently cancels the
   incomplete grant and activates refunds of unreleased escrow. The first non-quorum
   outcome already cancels after its one seven-day grace window expires.

Silence is never approval. Non-voter funds never go to protocol reserve or automatically
to the author.

## Proof Packages

Before a proposal may enter a V2.3 round, its author must commit an immutable milestone
plan URI and content hash while the proposal is still pending. The plan describes both
milestones, their acceptance criteria, expected proof types, and the fixed 20/40/40
schedule.

Each later proof submission includes:

- one `proofURI`, preferably an IPFS CID or another content-addressed pointer;
- a `proofHash` for the referenced package;
- a concise immutable summary for user interfaces and indexers.

The package may include repository commits, deployment addresses, test reports, a demo,
design deliverables, invoices, and other supporting links. The contract stores the
commitment, not every document or personal datum.

An off-chain expert may review a package for an individual backer. That expert has no
protocol privilege and cannot cast a vote on the backer's behalf unless a future delegated
voting design is explicitly added.

## Voter-Scale Refactor

The current `VotingSystemUpgradeable` limits an idea to 30 voters because
`endVotingRound` loops over all winning voters to call `VoterProgression`.

V2.3 removes this dependency from settlement:

1. `endVotingRound` continues to select the winner, update idea state, and settle funding.
2. It does not iterate through the winning cohort or make a per-voter external call.
3. A winning backer can later call `claimWinningParticipation(roundId)` to receive their
   non-financial VoterProgression credit.
4. The claim checks that the caller pledged to the settled winner and has not claimed the
   credit before.

This makes settlement gas bounded by the fixed number of ideas in the round rather than
the number of backers. The hard 30-voter cap can then be removed for new V2.3 rounds.
Backer lists must be displayed through paginated indexer queries rather than an unbounded
on-chain array return.

## Legacy and Upgrade Safety

1. Existing V2.2 grants retain their reviewer-based rules. V2.3 rules apply only to
   rounds whose lifecycle mode was snapshotted as enabled at round start.
2. New state is appended only; existing proxy storage fields must never be reordered,
   removed, or repurposed.
3. The V2 upgrade procedure must begin with no active round, or use an explicit round
   version boundary that prevents changing rules mid-round.
4. V2.3 activation requires the explicit `configure-v2-3-backer-milestones.ts`
   post-upgrade configuration and on-chain postflight checks. The VotingSystem must
   remain paused while it enables verified-human voting, sets the 14-day default, and
   enables V2.3 only for subsequently started rounds; the script refuses to change
   those rules while a legacy round remains active.
5. Existing refunds, unclaimed-grant expiry, and legacy author bonds must remain readable
   and claimable after the upgrade.
6. A deployment with a pre-V2.3 test queue may use the one-time
   `retire-v2-3-legacy-queue.ts` migration while VotingSystem is paused. It only retires
   the immutable ID range below `firstConditionalFundingIdeaId`, requires every such idea
   to still be `Pending`, returns each author bond, marks those ideas `Cancelled`, and moves
   `lastUsedIdeaId` to the fixed boundary minus one. It cannot skip any V2.3 proposal or
   alter the strictly sequential selection of later rounds.

## Contract Work

| Module | Required V2.3 work |
| --- | --- |
| `GrantManagerUpgradeable` | Backer milestone state, weighted vote accounting, one-resubmission guard, 20/40/40 payouts, permissionless finalization, legacy/new round boundary |
| `FundingPoolUpgradeable` | Progressive fee release, restoration of unvested fee into grant refunds, winning-cohort eligibility reads |
| `VotingSystemUpgradeable` | Three-backer viability rule, remove settlement loop and 30-voter cap, lazy winning-progression claims |
| `VoterProgressionUpgradeable` | Permit the VotingSystem's per-user lazy progression registration without duplicate credit |
| `IdeaRegistryUpgradeable` | Immutable milestone plan URI/hash for new funding proposals |
| Interfaces / errors / events | New ABI, read methods, proof, vote, finalization, expiry, fee-vesting and lazy-claim events |

## Frontend, Indexing, and Backend Work

- The idea page lets an author commit the immutable milestone plan URI/hash in a
  separate transaction before the pending idea may enter a V2.3 round.
- Grant pages show tranche accounting, proof packages, vote weight, quorum progress,
  approval progress, review deadline, grace state, and refund state.
- Winning pledgers can cast or change their milestone vote and claim their progression
  credit.
- The subgraph indexes plans, proof submissions, backer votes, vote changes, finalization,
  progressive fees, cancellations, refunds, and progression claims.
- The backend may send notifications but never decides an on-chain outcome.

## Required Test Matrix

1. Storage layout validation from V2.2 proxy state to V2.3 implementations.
2. Winner selection where a higher pledge fails its funding target or three-backer rule.
3. Successful 20/40/40 grant with progressive 5% fee vesting.
4. Author claim expiry restores full winning gross pledge.
5. Author misses each milestone submission deadline.
6. Quorum and approval edge cases, including exact threshold rounding.
7. Vote changes update yes/no weight and unique approve counts correctly.
8. One approve vote, one whale, author vote, duplicate vote, and non-winning pledger vote
   all fail where required.
9. Non-quorum at 14 days, grace period, and final cancellation/refund.
10. First rejection, valid resubmission, second rejection, and second non-quorum.
11. Refunds after each stopped tranche preserve total accounting.
12. Lazy progression claims cannot double-count, cannot be claimed by a losing pledger,
    and do not affect settlement gas as cohort size grows.
13. Pause, reentrancy, role, zero-address, malformed proof, and boundary-time tests.
14. Slither, gas profiling, an Arc fork upgrade rehearsal, and a full frontend/subgraph
    end-to-end test before any live Arc upgrade.

## Explicit Non-Goals for V2.3

- A paid global reviewer marketplace.
- A mandatory performance bond beyond the existing anti-spam bond.
- Hidden reviewer fees.
- Automatic payout on backer inactivity.
- Secret ballots, MACI, bribery-proof voting, delegated voting, or arbitration.
- Changing V3 Community governance semantics.
