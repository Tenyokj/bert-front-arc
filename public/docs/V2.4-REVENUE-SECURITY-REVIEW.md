### Reading the High Count

The twelve High results are two detector families, not twelve reproduced thefts:

- Six `arbitrary-send-erc20` results: two FundingPool intake functions and four
  existing CommunityTreasury intake functions (membership, proposal bonds,
  binary votes and slate votes). These deliberately consume a specified user's
  allowance when called by an authorized system module. An allowance alone does
  not let any outsider call these restricted functions. Module/role compromise
  remains a real trust risk, not something this revenue update fixes.
- Six `reentrancy-balance` results in three new functions: pool collection,
  Treasury collection and beneficiary payout. Several results flag different
  pre-transfer variables in the same function. The code intentionally reads
  balances before/after transfer to reject taxes or surcharges. `nonReentrant`
  and effects-before-transfer protect the mutating paths; callback and rollback
  tests exercise this boundary. A detector warning is not itself a successful
  callback exploit, and these tests are not a proof against every possible token.

No unrestricted theft path was reproduced by this scoped review. That is not
equivalent to a full independent audit or permission to ignore the remaining
Medium/Low findings. We retain the findings and reasoning rather than removing
balance checks or muting detectors just to obtain a green report.

## Manual Financial Boundaries

1. An old round has zero revenue share even after a new policy activates.
2. Only earned fees mint credit, never pool balance differences, bonds, reserve
   contributions, direct donations or grant/refund capital.
   Recorded winning pledges fix the eligible fee base at settlement; reserve
   allocation before round opening is explicitly regression-tested and excluded.
3. Credit stays outside `protocolReserve`; reserve allocation cannot spend it.
4. Collection pays its caller's own entitlement, with no caller-chosen destination.
5. Rotation/disable cannot revoke or redirect an already snapshotted receiver.
6. Treasury has fixed beneficiaries, no owner, no proxy and no rescue/approval API.
7. Failed token transfers revert ledger consumption; recipient tax and sender
   surcharge are rejected. Receiver/beneficiary failure is isolated to its pull call.
8. The token is permanently pinned after a nonzero policy is scheduled, even if
   cancelled, to prevent historical liabilities from changing denomination.
9. `syncBalance` cannot subtract reserve again, mint revenue from surplus or
   silently write off an actual custody shortfall.

- The script stops **before** a local proxy upgrade, policy activation or new
  revenue flow. No public transaction or public wallet signer is used.

This establishes an asset-replay limitation of generic EDR, not a reproduced
FundingPool exploit on Arc. This failed run remains historical evidence; the
